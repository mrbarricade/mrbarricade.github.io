San Jose's speed cameras start December 14. Here is how they work.

By Vignesh Swaminathan, PE

If you drive in San Jose, you may have noticed new poles going up. The city is starting its speed safety camera program, and warnings begin on December 14, 2026.

Why now

A 2023 state law, AB 645, lets six California cities test speed cameras: San Jose, San Francisco, Oakland, Los Angeles, Long Beach and Glendale. Each city can run its pilot for up to five years, and the law ends on January 1, 2032 unless it is extended. Cities have to report on how well it worked.

How it works

  • San Jose has 33 camera locations, with at least one in every council district.
  • Sites were chosen based on crash history and closeness to places like hospitals and senior centers.
  • A camera only triggers when a car is going 11 mph or more over the posted limit.
  • For the first 60 days, drivers get warning notices only. New camera locations added later get their own 60 day warning period too.
  • Notices go to the vehicle's registered owner.

What a camera can and cannot do

Cameras change behavior fast where they stand. But speeding is mostly a design problem. Wide lanes, long straight stretches and big sweeping corners tell drivers it is fine to go fast, no matter what the sign says.

That is why the data from these cameras is valuable. It shows exactly where speeding keeps happening. Those are the streets where design changes, like narrower lanes, raised crossings and tighter corners, can make slower driving feel normal even without a camera.

What you can do

Pay attention to where the cameras are and why. If your street has a speeding problem and no camera, tell your council office. Speed data and crash history are what move streets up the list.
