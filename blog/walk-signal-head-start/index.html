The walk signal head start

By Vignesh Swaminathan, PE

Have you ever noticed the walk signal turning on a few seconds before the cars get green? That is not a glitch. It is called a leading pedestrian interval, and it is one of the simplest safety upgrades a city can make.

How it works. For a few seconds, usually three to seven, people walking get the WALK signal while cars on the same street are still held at red. By the time drivers get green, you are already in the crosswalk, right in front of them where they can see you.

Why it matters. The most common conflict at a signal is a driver turning into the crosswalk while looking for a gap in traffic. A head start puts you in their line of sight before they start the turn. The Federal Highway Administration lists it as a proven safety countermeasure and reports that it reduces crashes between cars and people walking at intersections.

Who it helps most. Kids walking to school, older adults, people using wheelchairs or walkers, and anyone who needs a moment longer to step off the curb.

Why it is easy to add. At many intersections it is mostly a change to the signal timing, which makes it one of the more affordable safety tools a city has. Some intersections also need audible signals so people who are blind or have low vision get the same head start.

What you can do: if a busy crossing near you does not have one, ask your city. Name the intersection and describe the turning traffic you see. Sometimes it really is just a timing change.
