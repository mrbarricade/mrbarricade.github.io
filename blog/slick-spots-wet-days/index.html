Rain is coming. Here is how to stay upright on a bike or scooter

By Vignesh Swaminathan, PE

The first rain after a dry stretch is when streets feel different. Oil and dust mix with water, and some surfaces get slick. If you ride a bike or scooter, a few spots deserve extra respect.

Where it gets slippery

  • Big painted symbols and crosswalk stripes, especially large areas of paint or plastic marking material.
  • Metal covers, like utility lids and the steel plates crews leave after a repair.
  • Leaves, especially wet ones, piled in bike lanes or near drains.

The risk is highest when you are doing something other than riding straight: braking hard, turning or crossing at an angle.

What riders can do

  • Brake earlier and more gently than you do in the dry.
  • Take turns wide and upright, and avoid leaning hard.
  • Cross metal covers and painted symbols at a right angle, not along them.
  • Keep your lights on. Rain and darkness make you harder to see.

What engineers and cities can do

Designers can keep large markings out of the spots where bike tires roll and place metal covers outside the bike lane when possible. Markings can also include materials that add grip. After the first storms, crews can check problem spots and clear leaves from bike lanes and drains.

None of this is glamorous, but it is the kind of detail that decides whether a bike lane feels safe in winter.

What you can do

If you find a slick spot, a loose plate or a bike lane full of leaves, report it to your city's public works or 311 app. Include the location and what you saw. Most cities fix these quickly once they know.
