What does a traffic engineer do?

By Vignesh Swaminathan, PE (Mr. Barricade)

A traffic engineer plans, designs, studies, and operates streets so people and goods move safely and efficiently. Day to day that means timing traffic signals, studying speeds and crashes, deciding where signs, markings, and crosswalks go, and reviewing how new development affects the street network.

The official definition

California's engineering licensing board defines traffic engineering as the branch of engineering that understands the science of measuring traffic and travel and the human factors behind traffic, and applies it to planning, operating, and evaluating streets and highways, the land next to them, and other modes of travel, to provide safe and efficient movement of people and goods.

What the work looks like

The board's traffic engineer exam breaks the work into four areas: planning (27 percent), design (28 percent), studies and analyses (18 percent), and operations (27 percent). In practice that covers things like:

  • Signal timing and left turn phasing. See why traffic lights take so long.
  • Speed studies and speed limits. See who decides the speed limit.
  • Crossings, signs, and markings under the MUTCD.
  • Traffic impact reviews for new development, and safety analysis of crash patterns.

Traffic engineer versus civil engineer

In California, civil engineering is a practice act, so only licensed civil engineers may practice it. Traffic engineering is a title act: only someone licensed as a traffic engineer may use that title, but the work itself does not require that specific license, and civil engineers often perform traffic engineering tasks as part of their work. Other states license engineers differently, so check your state board's rules.

What is a traffic study?

A traffic study, or engineering study, is how a traffic engineer turns a concern into evidence. It can include traffic and pedestrian counts, speed measurements, crash history, sight distance checks, and field observation. The MUTCD requires an engineering study before a new traffic signal is installed and before a speed zone is set, and it is the normal first step for crosswalk and traffic calming requests too.

How to hire one

  • For a public street concern, start with your city or county traffic engineer through 311 or the transportation department. Reviewing public streets is their job.
  • For a private project, such as a development or a driveway, hire a licensed professional engineer with traffic experience, and confirm the license with your state's engineering board.
  • Ask for examples of similar studies and who will sign the work.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a traffic engineer and a civil engineer?

Traffic engineering focuses on how streets operate and how people move on them. In California, civil engineering is a practice act requiring a civil license, while traffic engineering is a title act: only licensed traffic engineers may use the title, and civil engineers often do traffic engineering work.

How do I hire a traffic engineer?

For public streets, contact your city or county traffic engineer first. For private projects, hire a licensed professional engineer with traffic experience and verify the license with your state engineering board.

What is a traffic study?

An engineering study that collects counts, speeds, crash history, and field observations to decide whether a change like a signal, speed limit, crosswalk, or traffic calming is justified. The MUTCD requires one before installing a traffic signal or setting a speed zone.
