Are bike lanes safe?

By Vignesh Swaminathan, PE (Mr. Barricade)

The research says bike lanes are associated with fewer crashes, and physical separation matters most. In FHWA research, bike lanes separated by flexible posts had about 53 percent fewer bike crashes than painted or painted buffer lanes. Adding bike lanes to urban four lane undivided collector and local roads in Texas was associated with about 49 percent fewer total crashes.

Separation: the 2023 FHWA study

FHWA's 2023 study used crash data from Cambridge, San Francisco, and Seattle, then checked the results against Austin and Denver. Compared with a traditional or painted buffer bike lane, a separated lane with flexible posts had a crash modification factor of 0.468, about 53 percent fewer bike crashes. Posts blended with other vertical elements came out at 0.602, about 40 percent fewer. A painted buffer alone did not show a statistically significant change compared with a traditional lane.

Adding bike lanes: the 2021 FHWA study

FHWA also studied adding bike lanes by narrowing travel lanes or shoulders. In Texas data, it was associated with about 49 percent fewer total crashes on four lane undivided urban collectors and local roads, and about 27 percent fewer on two lane roads, assuming no change in bike traffic. If bike traffic rose 20 percent, the two lane estimate was about 31 percent. Data from Washington State had a smaller sample and showed no statistically significant change.

What other researchers found

  • NACTO cites research finding protected bike lanes can decrease risk by 90 percent compared with a major street with no bike facility.
  • IIHS notes the 2023 FHWA finding that separated lanes are more safety effective than painted ones, and its own 2020 study found risk varies by design. Fewer driveway and street crossings, more continuous separation, and simpler crossings for drivers lowered risk.

That last point is why intersections matter so much. See what a protected intersection is.

A recent federal policy change

In July 2026, FHWA removed bicycle lanes from its list of Proven Safety Countermeasures, which NPR reported went from 28 items to 23. FHWA said the change was part of a review to align the list with current DOT policies. The research reports cited on this page remain published on FHWA's websites.

Frequently asked questions

Are protected bike lanes safer than painted ones?

In FHWA's 2023 study, lanes separated by flexible posts had about 53 percent fewer bike crashes than traditional or painted buffer lanes.

Are painted bike lanes safe?

Adding bike lanes was associated with fewer total crashes in FHWA's Texas data. Paint alone does less than physical separation, and a painted buffer alone did not show a significant difference in FHWA's 2023 study.

Are bike lanes still an FHWA Proven Safety Countermeasure?

No. FHWA removed bicycle lanes from that list in July 2026. Its research reports on bike lane safety remain published.
