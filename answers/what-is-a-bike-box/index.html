What is a bike box, and how do you use it?

By Vignesh Swaminathan, PE (Mr. Barricade)

A bike box is a marked waiting area at a traffic signal where people on bikes stop in front of the cars on a red light. Drivers stop at a second stop line set back from the intersection, and bikes wait in the space ahead of it, where drivers can see them when the light turns green. Under FHWA's 2016 interim approval, the box is at least 10 feet deep, used only at signals, and right turns on red are banned on that approach.

How to use a bike box

  • On a bike: when the light is red, ride up the bike lane into the box and wait ahead of the stopped cars. The box exists so you are visible at the start of green.
  • Driving: stop at the first stop line, behind the box. FHWA's design calls for a STOP HERE ON RED sign with an EXCEPT BICYCLES plaque at that line.
  • Turning right: no turn on red from that approach. FHWA requires a NO TURN ON RED sign wherever a bike box is installed.

How a bike box is designed

FHWA's interim approval for bike boxes, IA-18 from October 2016, sets these conditions:

  • Only at signalized intersections.
  • An advance stop line at least 10 feet before the intersection stop line, with at least one bike symbol in the box.
  • At least 50 feet of bike lane leading into the box, so riders do not have to squeeze between cars to reach it.
  • Countdown pedestrian signals when the box spans more than one lane, so riders can tell whether there is time to move across before the light turns green.
  • Green paint is optional inside the box and on the approach.

Do bike boxes work?

FHWA reviewed more than 25 experiments, including ones in Austin, Columbus, Madison, Portland, and Washington, DC. It documented fewer conflicts between people on bikes and turning drivers, fewer avoidance maneuvers, and fewer bikes and cars stopping in the crosswalk. Surveys showed both drivers and riders understood how to use the box. IIHS also notes research showing bike boxes reduce conflicts with vehicles at signals.

Turning left across several lanes is a different problem. For that, see the two stage turn box.

Frequently asked questions

Can cars stop in the bike box?

No. Drivers stop at the advance stop line behind the box. The box is the waiting area for people on bikes during the red light.

Can you turn right on red at a bike box?

No. FHWA's interim approval requires turns on red to be prohibited, with a NO TURN ON RED sign, on any approach that has a bike box.

Is a bike box the same as a two stage turn box?

No. A bike box puts riders ahead of cars on the near side of the intersection. A two stage turn box sits on the far side and is used to turn left in two steps.
