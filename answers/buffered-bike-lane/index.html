What is a buffered bike lane?

By Vignesh Swaminathan, PE (Mr. Barricade)

A buffered bike lane is a regular bike lane with an extra painted buffer, usually hatched lines, between the bike lane and moving cars or parked cars. Arlington County, Virginia describes that buffer as typically adding 1 to 3 feet of space. It is still paint, so nothing physical stops a driver from crossing it.

Paint, buffer, or protection

A conventional bike lane is a lane line plus bike markings. A buffered lane adds hatched markings beside it. A protected, or separated, bike lane goes one step further: NACTO and FHWA both define it as having a buffer and a vertical element, such as a curb, a median, or flexible posts.

What the buffer does

Arlington County's safety toolbox lists what a buffer adds:

  • A wider passing distance between people biking and drivers.
  • Room to pass another bike without entering a car lane.
  • A way to ride outside the door zone when the buffer sits next to parked cars.
  • Better sightlines for drivers at cross streets and driveways.

The county also cautions that a buffer should not be so wide that drivers mistake it for a parking or travel lane.

Where it fits

Arlington applies buffered lanes to minor arterials and local streets with posted speed limits below 35 mph, and where a separated lane is not feasible. NACTO says protected bike lanes should be explored on streets with more than 6,000 vehicles a day, speeds over 25 mph, or more than one travel lane in each direction.

What the crash research shows

In FHWA's 2023 study of bike lanes in Cambridge, San Francisco, and Seattle, a painted buffer alone did not show a statistically significant difference in bike crashes compared with a traditional bike lane. Adding flexible posts to a buffered lane did: the study estimated about 56 percent fewer bike crashes (crash modification factor 0.441). Arlington notes research finding that buffered lanes are perceived as safer by people biking, drivers, and practitioners.

My engineering view: a buffer is useful, and it is also the space where posts or curbs can go later. See what a protected bike lane is.

What you can do

  • If your street has a buffered lane that drivers cross often, report it through 311 or your city's transportation department.
  • Ask whether vertical separation could be added, for example at the next repaving.

Frequently asked questions

How wide is the buffer in a buffered bike lane?

Arlington County describes the hatched buffer as typically adding 1 to 3 feet of space between people biking and vehicles or parking.

Is a buffered bike lane safer than a regular bike lane?

It gives riders more space and is perceived as safer, but in FHWA's 2023 study a painted buffer alone did not show a statistically significant difference in bike crashes. Adding flexible posts was associated with about 56 percent fewer bike crashes than a buffered lane.

What is the difference between a buffered and a protected bike lane?

A buffered lane is separated by paint only. A protected lane has a buffer plus a vertical element such as a curb, median, or flexible posts.
