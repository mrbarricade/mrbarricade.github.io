How do bike traffic signals work?

By Vignesh Swaminathan, PE (Mr. Barricade)

A bike signal is a traffic light with bicycle shaped red, yellow, and green lenses that applies only to people on bikes. It lets an agency give bikes their own time: a head start before turning cars, called a leading bike interval, or a fully separate phase while turning traffic waits. FHWA allowed bike signal faces in a December 2013 interim approval, IA-16.

What the lights mean

Under IA-16, red, yellow, and green bike indications mean the same thing as a regular red, yellow, and green, but only for people on bikes. If a bike signal faces you, follow it. The key rule is that a bike signal may only be used where bikes moving on green or yellow are not in conflict with any car movement at the same time, including turns on red.

Three ways to time a bike signal

NACTO's Don't Give Up at the Intersection describes the main options:

  • Leading bike interval. Bikes get a head start in front of turning vehicles, which gives riders a priority position in the intersection.
  • Protected then permissive. Bikes and through traffic start together, then turning drivers get a flashing yellow arrow and must yield. NACTO says this fits streets with moderate to high turn volumes and speeds of 25 mph or below.
  • Fully protected bike phase. Bikes and people walking go while turning cars hold on a red arrow, then turning cars go while bikes hold. NACTO points to this where turns exceed about 150 per hour, speeds are 30 mph or higher, drivers yield poorly, or several lanes turn across the bikeway.

Does it make a difference?

FHWA reviewed experiments in places including Denver, Long Beach, Minneapolis, Washington, DC, and Arlington County, Virginia. It found people on bikes adjusted quickly and complied more with bike signals than with other devices. Depending on how the signal was used, the research showed fewer bike crashes overall, or crash rates up to 45 percent lower where bike volumes grew at the same time.

Where you see them

Bike signals pair naturally with a protected intersection, where bikes already have their own path through the corner.

Frequently asked questions

Do I follow the bike signal or the regular signal?

If a bike signal faces you, follow it. Under FHWA's interim approval, its indications apply only to people on bikes.

What is a leading bike interval?

A head start for people on bikes before turning vehicles go, which NACTO says gives riders a priority position in the intersection.

Why do some intersections have bike signals?

To separate bikes from turning traffic in time. NACTO recommends fully separate bike phases at high turn volumes, higher speeds, or where several lanes turn across the bikeway.
