Why 511

Two numbers every resident should know.

Dial 511 and you learn how your streets are working right now. Dial 311 and you can start changing them. Mr. Barricade 511 lives in that gap.

511

Travel info

In July 2000 the FCC set aside 511 as the national number for traveler information. Most states and regions now use it for traffic, transit, road closures and construction. In the Bay Area, 511.org is run by MTC and covers transit schedules, trip planning, bicycling, carpooling and FasTrak. It's what you check before you go.

511.org

311

Your city

311 connects you to non emergency city services. Baltimore launched the first in 1996, and Chicago built the first full service request system in 1999. San Jose, San Francisco, Oakland and hundreds of other places have one now. Use it for potholes, broken streetlights, faded crosswalks, dark signals and debris in the road.

"Burning building? Call 911. Burning question? Call 311." City of Akron

How to file a 311 report that gets fixed

  1. Pin the exact spot: an address or the nearest intersection, and which corner.
  2. Add a photo, ideally with something for scale.
  3. Say what's wrong and who it puts at risk: kids walking to school, people on bikes, wheelchair users.
  4. Save the ticket number.
  5. If it keeps happening, bring your ticket numbers to public comment. One report fixes a hazard. A pattern of reports gets a street onto a project list.

About Vignesh

Watch: How I became a TikToker

Vignesh Swaminathan, PE, is a licensed civil engineer working on protected bike lanes and safer streets, known online as Mr. Barricade. He has 11 years of experience in active transportation and highway design.

He previously founded a transportation design firm and has designed and delivered more than 100 protected corners in California with local governments, agencies and Caltrans. He took a leading role in writing NACTO's Don't Give Up at the Intersection design guide.

He was Lead Engineer on tunnel projects for The Boring Company in Las Vegas, operating tunnel boring machines and designing stations and alignments.

Today he reaches 1.6M people as @mrbarricade, explaining how streets get designed and how you can change yours.
