Bike lanes

Bike lanes explained: every type, rule, and marking

By Vignesh Swaminathan, PE (Mr. Barricade)

A bike lane is part of the street set aside for people on bikes. A painted lane is marked with a line and a bike symbol, a buffered lane adds a painted gap, and a protected bike lane adds a physical barrier like a curb or flexible posts. FHWA research found lanes separated with flexible posts had about 53 percent fewer bike crashes than painted or buffered lanes in the cities studied.

Types of bike lanes

TypeWhat it is
Conventional bike lanePart of the road marked for bikes with a white lane line plus bike symbol and arrow markings.
Buffered bike laneA bike lane with a painted buffer, typically adding 1 to 3 feet of space between people biking and cars or parking.
Protected (separated) bike laneSeparated from car lanes by both a buffer and a vertical element such as a curb, median, or flexible posts.
Two way protected bike laneBoth directions of bike traffic share one protected lane, usually on one side of the street.
Shared lane (sharrow)No separate lane. A marking shows where people biking should ride in a lane shared with cars.

NACTO recommends exploring protected bike lanes on streets with more than 6,000 vehicles a day, speeds above 25 mph, or more than one travel lane in each direction. More on protected bike lanes.

What the markings mean

  • Green paint marks bike lanes and the spots where cars and bikes cross. FHWA gave it interim approval in 2011. Why green?
  • Bike box: a waiting area at least 10 feet deep in front of the car stop line, used only at signals. How to use one
  • Two stage turn box: a box on the far side of the intersection for turning left in two steps. How it works
  • Sharrow: a shared lane marking, not a bike lane. Sharrow vs bike lane
  • Crossbike: dotted lines, sometimes filled with green, that carry the bike lane through the intersection.

Rules for drivers and riders

Rules depend on your state. In California, drivers may enter a bike lane only to park where parking is allowed, to enter or leave the road, or to prepare for a turn within 200 feet of the intersection (Vehicle Code 21209). People biking slower than traffic must use the lane, with exceptions such as passing, turning left, and avoiding hazards (Vehicle Code 21208). Full answer

Do bike lanes make streets safer?

The research points to separation. In FHWA research, adding bike lanes by narrowing lanes or shoulders on urban four lane undivided collectors and local roads was associated with about 49 percent fewer total crashes in Texas data. Separating a bike lane with flexible posts was associated with about 53 percent fewer bike crashes. FHWA removed bicycle lanes from its Proven Safety Countermeasures list in July 2026; the research reports cited here remain published. See the research

Frequently asked questions

What is a bike lane?

A bike lane is part of the roadway set aside for people on bikes, marked with a white line and a bike symbol. A protected bike lane adds a physical barrier such as a curb or posts.

Why are bike lanes green?

Green pavement marks where people on bikes are expected to ride and where bikes and cars cross paths. FHWA gave green interim approval for this use in 2011 after experiments showed riders positioned themselves more accurately and drivers were more aware of bikes. Blue was ruled out because it is used for accessible parking.

Are protected bike lanes safer than painted ones?

FHWA research found that bike lanes separated with flexible posts had about 53 percent fewer bike crashes than traditional or painted buffer lanes in the cities studied. NACTO cites research finding protected lanes can decrease risk by 90 percent compared with a major street with no bike facility.

Can cars drive in a bike lane?

Only in limited cases. In California, Vehicle Code 21209 lets drivers enter a bike lane to park where parking is allowed, to enter or leave the road, or to prepare for a turn within 200 feet of the intersection. Rules differ by state.

Do you have to ride in the bike lane?

In California, Vehicle Code 21208 requires people biking slower than traffic to use the bike lane, with exceptions for passing, left turns, avoiding hazards, and approaching a right turn. Other states differ.

What is a bike box?

A bike box is the space between an advance stop line and the intersection stop line where people on bikes wait ahead of cars at a red light. Under FHWA's interim approval it is used only at signals and comes with a no turn on red rule.

What is the difference between a bike lane and a sharrow?

A bike lane is space for bikes. A sharrow is a marking in a shared lane that shows where to ride. Sharrows were added to the national MUTCD in December 2009.

What is a protected intersection?

A protected intersection keeps bikes separated through the corner using a corner island, a set back crossing, and a bike waiting area. NACTO reports a San Francisco design where 98 percent of drivers yielded to people on bikes.
